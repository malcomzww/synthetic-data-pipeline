# Hygiene layer: detector recall against planted ground truth

Generated by `python scripts/generate_results.py`. Do not edit by hand.
Machine-specific provenance is in `results/environment.md` (gitignored).

## What this measures, and what it does not

**The contamination in this corpus was planted at a rate chosen here,
not discovered in the wild.** No claim is made about any real dataset.
The measurement is *detector recall against exact ground truth*: given
that 12% of records were spliced with a verbatim span
of the eval set, what fraction does a 13-gram overlap detector find?

**The generated records are templated, not model-generated.** No LLM was
called anywhere in this repo. See `src/synthetic_data_pipeline/generate.py`
for why measurability required that choice.

### Experiment parameters

- Seed: `7` (every stage derives from it; output is byte-stable)
- Generated records: 960 (900 base + planted copies)
- Eval set: 24 held-out items, public/hand-authored
- Planted near-duplicate pairs: 60
- Planted contamination: 108 records (11.2% of the corpus)
- Planted PII: 72 records (7.5%)
- Reproduce: `python scripts/generate_results.py`
- Model snapshot: none -- no model is called

## 1. The headline: contamination detected before decontamination

At the 13-gram threshold, of the 900 records that
reached the decontamination stage, **63 were flagged as
contaminated (7.0%)** against 108 planted.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| inspected at this stage | 900 |
| planted, still live here | 108 |
| flagged by the detector | 63 |
| **detector recall** | **58.3%** |
| **miss rate** | **41.7%** |
| precision | 100.0% |

**41.7% of records that genuinely contained a verbatim
eval span were not caught.** A decontamination pass reported as
complete would have shipped them.

That miss rate is not noise, and it is not a tuning failure. Split the
planted set by the length of the span that was spliced in:

| planted span | records | caught | missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| >= 13 tokens | 63 | 63 | 0 |
| < 13 tokens | 45 | 0 | 45 |

**Detectability is decided entirely by span length, with no exceptions
in either direction.** Every span of at least n tokens was found; every
shorter span was missed. That is not a discovered empirical fact so much
as the definition of the detector made visible -- an n-gram matcher
cannot see a fragment shorter than n -- but seeing it hold exactly is
what tells you the implementation does what it says.

The consequence is the useful one. **A clean decontamination report
means "no leaked span of 13+ tokens", not "no leakage".** Leakage
arrives in fragments too, and against those this method is not weak,
it is blind. Reporting the pass rate without that qualifier is how a
contaminated eval survives review.

### A second blind spot: unsegmented scripts

The span-length result above is measured. This one follows from the
same mechanism and is stated rather than measured, because the
multilingual slice here is too small to put a number on: the detector
tokenises on whitespace, and Chinese, Japanese and Thai do not use it
as a word boundary. A CJK record yields almost no word n-grams, so
word-level decontamination cannot see leakage in it at all. Any
multilingual corpus decontaminated this way is effectively
undecontaminated for a subset of its languages.

## 2. The 13 is a convention on a curve, not a cliff

`n` is inherited from the GPT-3 contamination analysis. Sweeping it
shows what the single number hides:

| n | flagged | recall | false positives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 108 | 100.0% | 0 |
| 8 | 99 | 91.7% | 0 |
| 13 **(default)** | 63 | 58.3% | 0 |
| 20 | 33 | 30.6% | 0 |

Recall falls monotonically as n rises, and it falls for exactly one
reason: each increment of n makes another band of planted spans too
short to match. Lowering n therefore *raises* recall on this corpus --
n=5 catches spans a 13-gram detector cannot.

So why not use n=5? Because the false-positive column here is
misleadingly clean. This eval set is 24 hand-authored items whose
phrasing is close to unique; against a real benchmark sharing ordinary
English with the training corpus, a 5-gram matcher flags large amounts
of uncontaminated text. **The cost of small n is invisible in this
fixture**, which is precisely why the 13 is taken on convention rather
than tuned to whatever maximises recall here. Tuning n on this corpus
would produce a number that does not survive contact with a real one.

## 4. Near-duplicate detection (MinHash + LSH)

At threshold 0.7, against 60 planted pairs:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| true positives | 57 |
| false negatives | 3 |
| false positives | 3066 |
| **recall** | **95.0%** |
| precision (lower bound) | 1.8% |

**That 1.8% precision is an artifact of the fixture, and it is
measurable rather than merely arguable.** Run the same detector over
the base corpus with *nothing planted at all*:

- near-duplicate pairs found with zero planted duplicates: **3,044**
- pairs found after planting 60: 3,123

The template generator emits records that differ by a single noun
phrase -- "Draft a checklist for reviewing *a token bucket*" against
"...*a write-ahead log*" -- and at Jaccard 0.7 those genuinely are
near-duplicates. The detector is right to flag them; the scoring
function calls them false positives only because they were not
*planted*. Roughly 3,044 of the 3,066
charged false positives are pairs that exist before any planting.

**So recall is the number to trust here and precision is not**, which
is a limitation of the fixture rather than a finding about MinHash. A
precision figure worth reporting needs a corpus whose true duplicate
set is known exhaustively, not just one whose planted subset is.

### Threshold sweep

| Jaccard threshold | recall | precision (lower bound) | detected pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 100.0% | 0.3% | 18,925 |
| 0.6 | 98.3% | 0.7% | 8,056 |
| 0.7 **(default)** | 95.0% | 1.8% | 3,123 |
| 0.8 | 75.0% | 42.9% | 105 |
| 0.9 | 11.7% | 100.0% | 7 |

Published instead of a single tuned threshold: a value chosen to
maximise F1 on this fixture would be an overfit, and reporting it as
*the* threshold is exactly the kind of number that does not transfer.

## 5. PII scrubbing

Against 72 records with a planted identifier:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| recall | 100.0% |
| precision | 100.0% |
| false negatives | 0 |
| false positives | 0 |

Scored **per record, not per span**: a record whose email was caught
but whose phone number was missed counts as a hit. That makes this an
optimistic metric, and it is named as one.

This measures whether the regexes fire on the shapes they were written
for. **It is not evidence that a corpus is PII-free.** Regex scrubbing
is structurally blind to unformatted PII -- a person's name, an address
in prose, a date of birth written as words. PII is scrubbed rather than
dropped, because removing every record containing an email address
would bias the corpus away from anything about contacting people.

## 6. Script coverage and code-switching

Measured on the 900-record base corpus, before anything is planted.
18 records carry a second script above the 15% threshold.
Dominant-script distribution:

| script | records |
|---|---|
| latin | 870 |
| arabic | 5 |
| cyrillic | 5 |
| devanagari | 5 |
| greek | 5 |
| han | 5 |
| hangul | 5 |

**This is script detection, not language identification.** It separates
Latin from Cyrillic from Han. It cannot tell Spanish from Portuguese,
because they share a script. Every language claim in this repo is a
claim about script, and no more. fastText's `lid.176.bin` would do
better and is a 126 MB download that makes a fresh clone fail offline;
that trade is stated rather than hidden.

## Limitations

- **Nothing here is contamination found in the wild.** The rate was
  chosen, planted, and then recovered. The transferable result is the
  detector's recall and miss rate, not the rate itself.
- **The corpus is templated.** Its lexical diversity is bounded by a
  cross product of 10 frames x 10 domains x 18 objects. A real
  model-generated corpus is more diverse in some ways and far more
  repetitive in others, and MinHash recall on it is not established here.
- **Word n-grams are close to blind on unsegmented scripts.** The CJK
  rows are carried through the pipeline but the 13-gram detector cannot
  see leakage in them. This repo does not establish a decontamination
  method that works for Chinese or Japanese.
- **PII recall is measured against planted regex-shaped identifiers**,
  which is close to the best case for a regex scrubber. Unformatted PII
  is not measured and not caught.
- **The eval set is 24 hand-authored items**, not a real benchmark. Its
  n-gram profile is not that of MMLU or GSM8K, and the false-positive
  rate against a real benchmark would differ.
- **No semantic dedup.** Records that mean the same thing in different
  words survive every stage here. Embedding-based dedup is the standard
  next step and is not implemented.
